## 2 Related Work

Difference with prior PLMs. Large-scale Transformer-based PLMs have recently achieved state-of-the-art performance on various natural language understanding and generation tasks [\(Devlin](#page-9-2) [et al.,](#page-9-2) [2019;](#page-9-2) [Yang et al.,](#page-11-0) [2019;](#page-11-0) [Radford et al.,](#page-10-1) [2019;](#page-10-1) [Liu et al.,](#page-10-2) [2019;](#page-10-2) [Keskar et al.,](#page-9-3) [2019\)](#page-9-3). Prior to Transformer-based PLMs, non-generative methods have seen some early success in pre-training sequence models for supervised downstream tasks including standard sequence auto-encoders [\(Dai](#page-9-7) [and Le,](#page-9-7) [2015;](#page-9-7) [Li et al.,](#page-10-7) [2015\)](#page-10-7), skip-thought models [\(Kiros et al.,](#page-9-8) [2015\)](#page-9-8) and paragraph vector

models [\(Le and Mikolov,](#page-10-8) [2014\)](#page-10-8) *etc.* However, all of these models do not generally learn a smooth, interpretable feature space for sentence encoding, or generating novel sentences. In this work, we aim to fill the gap to learn such a universal latent space in the field of Transformer-based PLMs.

Latent variable language modeling. Language VAEs have inspired new applications in NLP, via exploiting many interesting properties of the model's latent space [\(Bowman et al.,](#page-9-1) [2016;](#page-9-1) [Kim](#page-9-9) [et al.,](#page-9-9) [2018b\)](#page-9-9). Its modeling capacity and empirical performance is somewhat limited, partially due to the KL vanishing issue described in Section [4.3.](#page-4-0) Several attempts have been made to alleviate this issue, including different KL annealing/thresholding schemes [\(Bowman et al.,](#page-9-1) [2016;](#page-9-1) [Fu](#page-9-10) [et al.,](#page-9-10) [2019;](#page-9-10) [Higgins et al.,](#page-9-11) [2017;](#page-9-11) [Li et al.,](#page-10-9) [2019\)](#page-10-9), decoder architectures [\(Yang et al.,](#page-11-1) [2017;](#page-11-1) [Dieng](#page-9-12) [et al.,](#page-9-12) [2018\)](#page-9-12), auxiliary loss [\(Zhao et al.,](#page-11-2) [2017\)](#page-11-2), semi-amortized inference [\(Kim et al.,](#page-9-13) [2018a\)](#page-9-13), aggressive encoder training schedule [\(He et al.,](#page-9-14) [2019\)](#page-9-14), batch normalized inference [\(Zhu et al.,](#page-11-3) [2020\)](#page-11-3) and flexible posterior [\(Fang et al.,](#page-9-15) [2019\)](#page-9-15). [Subrama](#page-10-10)[nian et al.](#page-10-10) [\(2018\)](#page-10-10) have shown some promise that general encoder can benefit language generation. Transformers [\(Vaswani et al.,](#page-11-4) [2017\)](#page-11-4) are recently considered in VAEs for classification [\(Gururangan](#page-9-16) [et al.,](#page-9-16) [2019\)](#page-9-16) and storytelling [\(Wang and Wan,](#page-11-5) [2019\)](#page-11-5). Pre-training VAEs has been recently considered in conditional text generation to amortize the training of decoders and to allow easy adaptation in new generation tasks [\(Duan et al.,](#page-9-17) [2019\)](#page-9-17).

All these efforts utilize simple LSTM [\(Hochre](#page-9-6)[iter and Schmidhuber,](#page-9-6) [1997\)](#page-9-6) and shallow Transformer [\(Vaswani et al.,](#page-11-4) [2017\)](#page-11-4) architectures, thus with limited capacity. Our paper is the first big VAE model at the same scale of recent PLMs such as BERT and GPT-2. More importantly, we show that pre-training a meaningful latent space on a large text corpus can largely reduce the KL vanishing issue, and lead to new state-of-the-art performance.
